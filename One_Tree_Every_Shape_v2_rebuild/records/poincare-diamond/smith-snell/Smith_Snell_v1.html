The two charts side by side

Poincaré diamond: a stretch of line is a straight peel step; a face mixes the two books. Smith disk: a face is a straight peel step, r → r ⊕ Γ(z); a stretch of line turns the point around the centre (and pulls it in if lossy). Each one straightens what the other bends. Snell's job is to set z for the face: it keeps n sin θ the same on both sides, and that fixes the transverse impedances, Z ∝ 1/(n cos θ) for TE and cos θ / n for TM. At Brewster the TM face is z = 1, a peel step of zero, and the face disappears. Past the critical angle z is pure reactance, so the point rides the rim. Inside a thin gap the wave stops travelling and decays. Its step becomes real again, the same cosh/sinh cable law as the wound field.

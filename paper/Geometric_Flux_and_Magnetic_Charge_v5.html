Abstract

For the lower eigenprojector of the canonical two-level Pauli Hamiltonian, one Lean library now checks the full calculation from actual entrywise projector derivatives to curvature sinθ/2\sin\theta/2 and normalized flux 11. An equality theorem joins the two matrix encodings, so the curvature derivation and flux integral are connected inside the proof system. Three further results sharpen the geometry: pole obstruction for a scalar primitive with equal pole values, latitude-cut independence for the fixed north and south coefficients, and derived band-flux cancellation for the complementary projector. The unified library contains 56 theorem declarations and rejects 11 deliberately false controls. We distinguish these checked statements from the standard written eigenline-bundle interpretation, general coordinate and gauge invariance, and pullback degree theory. A magnetic-charge conclusion additionally requires a spatial domain map and a physical identification such as FB=κf*ΩF_B=\kappa f^*\Omega. The result is a continuous checked chain for this explicit example, with its mathematical and physical boundaries stated separately.

From a projector to a physical question

Start with an eigenline that changes as the Hamiltonian changes. Its curvature records the geometry of that family. Integrating the curvature can retain an integer even though no magnetic field in physical space has yet been specified. The question is what carries that integer from one domain to the other.

The two-level Pauli example lets us follow each step explicitly: matrix, derivative, curvature, flux, and physical interpretation. This revision closes the earlier written join between the curvature calculation and its integral. Both now live in one pinned Lean library, with a theorem identifying the matrix encodings and a theorem integrating the curvature built from the actual derivatives.

The new results are pole obstruction, latitude-cut independence and derived band-flux cancellation. These names describe what the code proves. Together they explain why the fixed two-chart calculation retains one when the latitude cut moves, and why selecting the other band reverses it. General bundle topology gives the familiar coordinate-independent Chern interpretation; constructing that interpretation is a separate mathematical task.

The underlying geometry is classical. Dirac’s construction concerns electromagnetic phase and magnetic charge ; Berry and Simon describe eigenstate phase and line-bundle geometry ; Wu and Yang explain the global connection picture . The example also appears in the author’s IQGT manuscript, Appendix B . This note’s contribution is a reproducible formal calculation and a clear account of the physical identification it needs.

We mark checked components and written proofs separately. The declaration map in Appendix A gives the exact formal coverage. All physical interpretations use the domains, conventions and hypotheses stated below.

Pole obstruction

The pole values expose the obstruction at the level actually encoded by the library.

Theorem 4.2 (Pole obstruction). [Lean-checked components]  There is no a:ℝ→ℝa:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R} such that a′(θ)=ω(θ)a'(\theta)=\omega(\theta) for every real θ\theta and a(0)=a(π)a(0)=a(\pi).

Proof. The derivative is continuous. The fundamental theorem of calculus and (9) give a(π)−a(0)=∫0πω(θ)dθ=1,a(\pi)-a(0)=\int_0^\pi\omega(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta=1, contradicting equal endpoint values. This is no_global_regular_gauge. ◻

For the axisymmetric ansatz A=a(θ)dϕA=a(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\phi, smoothness at each pole requires its angular coefficient to vanish there. One way to see the necessary condition is to integrate a smooth one-form around shrinking latitude circles: the integrals tend to zero, whereas the ansatz gives 2πa(θ)2\pi a(\theta). Thus a single smooth potential of this form would in particular have equal zero pole values, which the theorem excludes. This regularity argument is written geometry. The checked theorem quantifies over real functions and their derivatives, not arbitrary smooth one-forms on S2S^2.

The scalar functions aNa_N and aSa_S themselves are smooth for all real θ\theta. It is the regularity of adϕa\,\mathrm{d}\phi on the sphere that requires attention. The result supports the two-patch construction; it does not assert that a physical Dirac string must exist. In ordinary bundle language the regular local potentials avoid a singular global-potential representation.

Latitude-cut independence

Theorem 4.3 (Latitude-cut independence). (16)[Lean-checked components]  For every real cut θ0\theta_0, ∫0θ0ωdθ+∫θ0πωdθ=aN(θ0)−aS(θ0)=1.\int_0^{\theta_0}\omega\,\mathrm{d}\theta+ \int_{\theta_0}^{\pi}\omega\,\mathrm{d}\theta =a_N(\theta_0)-a_S(\theta_0)=1.

Proof. Use aN′=ωa_N'=\omega, aN(0)=0a_N(0)=0 for the first integral and aS′=ωa_S'=\omega, aS(π)=0a_S(\pi)=0 for the second. Their sum is aN(θ0)−aS(θ0)a_N(\theta_0)-a_S(\theta_0), which equals one. Lean proves the two equalities as flux_is_transition_jump and flux_one_at_every_cut. ◻

When 0≤θ0≤π0\leq\theta_0\leq\pi, the two terms are the normalized north- and south-cap contributions after azimuthal integration. Outside that interval, the same theorem is an oriented interval-integral identity. Moving the latitude cut leaves the sum fixed for the stated coefficients. This is narrower than invariance under arbitrary coordinate changes or arbitrary gauge transformations.

The standard topological interpretation comes from the transition phase eiϕe^{\mathrm{i}\phi} and its winding. A general local gauge change need not preserve a pointwise angular coefficient difference of one; the appropriate integrated transition invariant is the object that persists. The library checks the fixed coefficient identity and the cut-independent integral, while the transition map, its winding and the general invariance theorem remain written mathematics.

Derived band-flux cancellation

Let P+=I−P−P_+=I-P_-. Define C+C_+ exactly as in (7), using the actual entrywise derivatives of P+P_+.

Theorem 5.1 (Derived band-flux cancellation). (17)[Lean-checked components]  The complementary curvature and the two normalized fluxes satisfy C+(θ,ϕ)=−sinθ2,𝒞(C+)2π=−1,𝒞(C−)2π+𝒞(C+)2π=0.C_+(\theta,\phi)=-\frac{\sin\theta}{2},\qquad \frac{\mathcal C(C_+)}{2\pi}=-1,\qquad \frac{\mathcal C(C_-)}{2\pi}+\frac{\mathcal C(C_+)}{2\pi}=0.

Proof. The checked theorems hasDerivAt_compl_theta and hasDerivAt_compl_phi differentiate every entry of I−P−I-P_-: the derivatives are the negatives of those of P−P_-. Their two minus signs cancel in the commutator. Since the trace of a commutator is zero, iTr((I−P−)[V,W])=−iTr(P−[V,W]).\mathrm{i}\operatorname{Tr}\bigl((I-P_-)[V,W]\bigr) =-\mathrm{i}\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(P_-[V,W]\bigr). Lean combines the complement trace identity with the actual derivatives to prove derivedCurvatureCompl_eq. Integrating gives derived_compl_flux; combining it with (11) gives total_chern_zero. ◻

The last theorem’s historical name is retained for compatibility. Its statement is a zero sum of normalized real integrals, not a theorem about a constructed Chern-class object. Standard bundle theory interprets the selected eigenlines as carrying opposite first Chern numbers in a trivial ambient two-band bundle. Cancellation of the sum leaves each selected-band contribution nonzero. This revision checks that cancellation from both projectors’ actual derivatives, rather than merely integrating an independently supplied negative coefficient.

An extension obstruction at the degeneracy

At (18)d=0d=0, H(d)H(d) has no isolated lower eigenline. The obstruction can be seen without any Chern-class machinery. Restricting the normalized direction to the zz-axis gives P−(0,0,1)=(0001),P−(0,0,−1)=(1000).P_-(0,0,1)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad P_-(0,0,-1)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Theorem 6.1 (No continuous axis extension). [Lean-checked components]  Suppose F:ℝ→M2(ℂ)F:\mathbb{R}\to M_2(\mathbb{C}) satisfies F(t)=P−(0,0,1)F(t)=P_-(0,0,1) for t>0t>0 and F(t)=P−(0,0,−1)F(t)=P_-(0,0,-1) for t<0t<0. Then FF is not continuous at zero.

Proof. The real part of its (0,0)(0,0) entry is zero on the positive half-axis and one on the negative half-axis. Along tm=1/(m+1)t_m=1/(m+1) it tends to zero, and along −tm-t_m it tends to one. Both input sequences tend to zero. Continuity and uniqueness of limits would force F(0)00F(0)_{00} to have real part both zero and one, a contradiction. Lean proves the scalar jump obstruction and then applies it to the matrix coordinate. ◻

Any continuous three-dimensional extension of the normalized family that agrees with these axis values would restrict to such a continuous FF; hence it cannot exist. That restriction argument and the three-dimensional spectral-family construction are stated in writing, while Theorem 6.1 is the exact checked result. This is a parameter-space degeneracy obstruction, not an electromagnetic source equation.

The electromagnetic bridge

A spatial domain and a map

Let Σ\Sigma be a closed oriented physical spatial surface. A map f:Σ→S2f:\Sigma\to S^2 specifies how its points sample the Hamiltonian directions. It produces f*Ωf^*\Omega, but this pullback is still an eigenstate-geometric two-form unless it is identified with the electromagnetic field measured by a stated probe.

A particularly transparent sufficient identification is (19) FB|Σ=κf*Ω,F_B\big|_\Sigma=\kappa f^*\Omega, where κ\kappa is a nonzero dimensional coefficient. This equality is an additional hypothesis. A physical derivation must specify the coupling that makes the two curvatures the same observable, with consistent gauge transformations and normalization. Merely giving both quantities a connection or curvature notation does not supply that derivation.

Proposition 7.1 (Conditional pullback flux). (20)[Written proof; not formalized here]  If Σ≃S2\Sigma\simeq S^2, ff is smooth of degree dd, and (19) holds, then ΦB:=∫ΣFB=2πκd.\Phi_B:=\int_\Sigma F_B=2\pi\kappa d. More generally, put n=(2π)−1∫Σf*Ωn=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_\Sigma f^*\Omega whenever this record is justified. Then ΦB=qΦn\Phi_B=q_\Phi n with qΦ=2πκq_\Phi=2\pi\kappa.

Proof. The integral of the pullback of an oriented top-degree form under a smooth sphere map multiplies the integral by its degree. Apply that standard degree formula to (9) and multiply by κ\kappa. The second assertion is substitution. Neither the degree formula nor the pullback construction is formalized in the unified library. ◻

A constant map has d=0d=0, even though the original parameter-sphere eigenline has c1=1c_1=1. An orientation-preserving degree-one map retains the unit normalized flux. Specifying a domain map is therefore essential even before specifying the electromagnetic coupling.

For a probe of nonzero signed electric charge ee, a conventionally compatible physical identification of its dimensionless connection can set κ=ℏ/e\kappa=\hbar/e. Under that additional identification, (20) reads ΦB=(h/e)d\Phi_B=(h/e)d. This illustrates the usual Dirac flux unit; it neither derives the identification from the two-level projector nor reports an observed monopole. We keep flux separate from magnetic-source conventions that introduce factors such as μ0\mu_0.

An explicit exterior field

The arithmetic is elementary. The physical work lies in deriving the bridge for the system and probe under study.

Two useful companion facts are checked. First, a unit geometric record with zero magnetic flux cannot satisfy a nonzero-unit bridge. Second, for any chosen qΦq_\Phi and nn there exists the numerical bridge-compatible record (n,qΦn)(n,q_\Phi n). These are consistency checks for the numerical definitions.

The limits of a no-flux argument

There is also a precise boundary to an absence claim. If a globally defined smooth one-form AA exists on a closed oriented surface Σ\Sigma and FB=dAF_B=\mathrm{d}A there, Stokes’ theorem gives ∫ΣFB=0\int_\Sigma F_B=0. Likewise, if a smooth closed field extends over a ball bounded by Σ\Sigma, its boundary flux vanishes. These are written standard differential-form arguments, not theorems in this Lean package.

Their hypotheses matter. A nontrivial line bundle may require local potentials, and a punctured ball need not admit the extension required by the second argument. Local closedness dFB=0\mathrm{d}F_B=0 on the punctured exterior does not force all closed-surface fluxes there to vanish. The local charts in Section 4 demonstrate how a nonzero curvature integral can coexist with regular potentials on separate patches.

The code isolates the simplest logical version. Let (27)A0(r)A_0(r) mean Φ=0\Phi=0. It proves (∀r,A0(r)⇒¬M(r))and¬(∀r,¬M(r)).(\forall r,\ A_0(r)\Rightarrow\neg M(r)) \quad\text{and}\quad \neg(\forall r,\neg M(r)). The first statement restricts an admitted class. The second uses a record outside that class. Thus imposing zero flux as an admissibility condition is different from deriving a universal no-monopole theorem. The numerical statement isolates the distinction between restricting a class and deriving a property of every member of a physical theory.

Relation to projector transport and retained information

The example illustrates a methodological point relevant to a transport atlas: identify the object, base space and observation before treating two invariants as the same quantity. Several distinctions are already visible in this note.

A scalar summary can discard a distinction retained by a selected sector. Here the most immediate example is cancellation of two nonzero band fluxes in a trivial ambient bundle. That observation supports careful record selection; it does not identify Berry curvature, Hopf charge, instanton number or spectral flow with the same electromagnetic magnetic charge. Nor does the name “monopole” for an isotropic angular moment in a transport expansion perform such an identification.

For a light-transport, black-hole or neutrino model, the same discipline applies: specify the base space, retained sector, observable map and coupling before transferring an invariant. The present example supplies a worked template for that step.

What the checked calculation adds

The complete checked calculation starts with the explicit projector, differentiates its entries, computes the curvature, identifies that coefficient with the integral module’s definition, and obtains normalized flux one. Pole obstruction and latitude-cut independence then expose the fixed local-coefficient structure. Repeating the derivative calculation for the complementary band gives normalized flux minus one and exact cancellation.

The boundaries now have specific mathematical targets. First, construct the global eigenline bundle, its transition map and Chern class, and identify that class with the normalized curvature integral. This would turn the written topological interpretation into a checked one. Second, prove the appropriate invariance under general coordinate and gauge changes; moving the latitude cut alone is not that theorem. Third, construct spatial pullbacks and their degree formula. Finally, derive the physical field identification for a stated system and probe. That last step requires physical input even if all the preceding geometry is formalized.

The present calculation concerns the canonical degree-one Pauli sphere. A general smooth gapped two-level family can wrap that sphere with a different degree, including zero. Nonzero gap alone does not force unit flux. Neither a coordinate value of one nor the numerical bridge records assert the existence or absence of a magnetic particle.

For the transport atlas, the practical rule is precise: keep the invariant with its domain and observation map. Berry flux, Hopf charge, instanton number and an ℓ=0\ell=0 transport moment can each be useful while retaining different meanings. A successful bridge identifies the relevant structures and units in a particular system. That is where a geometric calculation becomes a testable magnetic-field claim.

Combined formal statement

The record-language theorem formal_stance, inherited from the original package, is 𝒩(ω)=1∧[(∃r:n(r)=1∧¬M(r))∧(∃r:n(r)=1∧M(r))]∧[∀qΦ≠0∀r:E(qΦ,r)⇒(M(r)⇔G(r))].\begin{aligned} &\mathcal N(\omega)=1\\[-0.3em] &\quad\land\bigl[(\exists r:\ n(r)=1\land\neg M(r)) \land(\exists r:\ n(r)=1\land M(r))\bigr]\\[-0.3em] &\quad\land\bigl[\forall q_\Phi\neq0\ \forall r:\ E(q_\Phi,r)\Rightarrow(M(r)\Longleftrightarrow G(r))\bigr]. \end{aligned} The first conjunct concerns a real integral. The second concerns freely assigned records. No formal map from that integral to the integer field of a record is asserted by the conjunction. In the physical application that interpretation is an additional construction, discussed in Section 4 and Section 7. The separate matrix_to_integer theorem now connects the actual matrix derivatives to the real integral, but it still constructs no map to the record’s integer field. The third conjunct is a transfer implication given a bridge. Reading all three as one unconditional physical-existence theorem would erase their different domains and hypotheses.
